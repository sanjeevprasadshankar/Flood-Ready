--- ### Why this exists I kept thinking about the same problem: by the time flood warnings reach a village with cut-off roads and no phone coverage, it's often too late to do anything useful with them. So for the Week 6 mini challenge I built FloodReady — a small end-to-end pipeline that watches river gauges, works out which villages are actually at risk (not just which gauges are), and gets a real SMS/WhatsApp/email out the door before things get bad. It's a dress rehearsal, not a finished product — but every piece of it actually runs, end to end, on real (synthetic but realistic) data. In short, it: 1. Scores flood risk at each of 6 river gauges using a 5-signal model 2. Ranks all 12 villages by a mix of flood risk *and* vulnerability 3. Writes plain-language alerts that tell people exactly what to do 4. Actually sends **SMS**, **WhatsApp**, and **Email** — not a mockup, real delivery --- ## 📁 What's in here | File | What it does | |------|-------------| | `floodready_setup.py` | The whole system, one file. This is what you run. | | `floodready_prototype.html` | Dashboard — just open it in a browser | | `floodready_village_profiles.csv` | Population, shelter capacity, road access per village | | `floodready_gauge_stations.csv` | Warning/danger thresholds for each gauge | | `floodready_river_readings.csv` | 60 days of sensor readings across the 6 gauges | | `logs/floodready_alerts.log` | Gets created automatically — every alert run is logged here | | `floodready_last_run.json` | Also auto-created — a snapshot of the most recent run | --- ## 🚀 Getting it running (~5 min) ### 1. Data pipeline (click to expand) ``` 3 CSV files │ ▼ Data Quality Fixes • 3 duplicate rows removed • River level 99.9m (sensor fault) → interpolated • Soil moisture 140% (impossible) → interpolated • Negative rainfall → set to 0 • 6 missing rainfall values → filled with median • 3 missing river levels → interpolated │ ▼ Feature Engineering • Level % of danger threshold • River rise rate (1-day diff) • Upstream level rise (leading indicator, ~1 day lag) • 3-day cumulative rainfall • Soil moisture saturation │ ▼ Risk Scoring (0–100) │ ▼ Village Prioritisation │ ▼ Alert Generation + Delivery ``` The raw sensor data wasn't clean going in — 8 separate issues needed fixing before any of this was trustworthy. That cleanup step isn't an afterthought; it's half the reason the risk scores are usable at all.
### Risk score — how a gauge goes from "readings" to "danger" Five signals, weighted like this: | Signal | Weight | Why it matters | |--------|--------|-----------| | River level % of danger threshold | 40% | The most direct signal — how close are we, right now | | River rise rate (m/day) | 20% | Speed matters as much as position | | Upstream level rise | 20% | A leading indicator — shows up roughly a day before the local gauge reacts | | 3-day cumulative rainfall | 10% | Captures sustained input into the basin | | Soil moisture % | 10% | Saturated ground stops absorbing, so runoff spikes | A score of **45 or above** flags a predicted flood. That threshold wasn't arbitrary — it's tuned deliberately (more on why below). ### Village priority — not just "who's flooding," but "who's most at risk" ``` Population + 2× vulnerable (elderly, children, disabled) → up to 35 pts Shelter capacity gap (population − shelter capacity) → up to 25 pts Road access: cut off = 30, seasonal = 15, all-weather = 0 Households without a phone (%) → up to 10 pts ``` **Final score = 60% flood risk + 40% priority.** The effect of this: a village with cut-off roads and spotty phone coverage climbs the list even when its raw flood risk number looks about the same as a better-connected village down the road. That's intentional — those are exactly the villages that are hardest to reach once things go wrong. ### Alert levels | Level | Score | What it means | |-------|-----------|--------| | 🔴 CRITICAL | ≥ 70 | Evacuate now — 12–24 hour window | | 🟠 HIGH | 45–69 | Start evacuating — 24–48 hours | | 🟡 MODERATE | 25–44 | Warn people, get ready | | 🟢 LOW | < 25 | Keep watching, no action yet | --- ## 📊 How well does it actually work? Tested on a held-out window (Aug 10–29, 2026), split by time so there's no leakage: | Metric | Value | What that means | |--------|-------|-------| | Recall | **86%** | Caught 6 out of 7 real floods | | Precision | 35% | About 3 false alarms per real flood | | F1 | 0.50 | — | | Missed floods | 1 | — | | Threshold | 45 | Deliberately tuned toward recall, not precision | **Why lean into false alarms instead of avoiding them?** Because missing a real flood can cost lives, and a false alarm just costs someone's time. The duty officer reviews every single alert before anything goes out — that human in the loop is what makes it safe to accept more false positives. --- ## 📨 Who gets what, and when | Channel | Goes to | Condition | |---------|-------------|------| | SMS | Village leader | Sent whenever a phone number is on file | | WhatsApp | Village group | Only if that's the village's preferred channel | | Email | Village leader | If an email address exists | | SMS | District officer | A rolled-up digest of every flagged village | | Email | District officer | Full HTML report, all the detail | One more thing: villages where 40%+ of households don't have a phone get a loudspeaker flag baked right into the alert, so the officer knows to send someone with a PA system instead of just relying on texts. --- ## 💸 What this costs Nothing, while you're building and testing. | Service | Free tier | Real-world cost | |---------|-----------|-------------------| | Twilio SMS | ~15 trial messages | ~₹0.65/SMS in India | | Twilio WhatsApp | Free sandbox | ~₹0.50–1/message | | SendGrid | 100 emails/day, forever | Free, for this volume | | Python + cron | Free | Free | For 12 villages sending 2 alerts a day, production would run **under ₹50/month**. Genuinely cheap for what it does. --- ## 🗂️ Inside `floodready_setup.py` It's one file, but it's organized into 13 sections so you're never hunting around: ``` 0 CONFIG block — your API keys and settings 1 INSTALLER — pip installs everything 2 CONFIG WIZARD — setup walkthrough 3 SMS / WhatsApp / Email senders 4 Message builders (SMS text + HTML email) 5 Logging — writes to logs/floodready_alerts.log 6 Main pipeline — the full run() function 7 Test sender 8 Scheduler — runs every N minutes 9 CSV refresh — recomputes live data 10 Dashboard launcher 11 Cron setup instructions (Linux/Mac/Windows) 12 Status checker 13 CLI entry point ``` --- ## 🤝 A few things I was careful about **Missed floods vs. false alarms** — I set the threshold at 45 specifically to push recall up to 86%, and backed that with a hard rule: nothing goes out without the duty officer reviewing it first. No SMS fires on its own. **Fairness in who gets prioritized** — Villages with bad roads and low phone coverage score *higher*, not lower. A 720-person village where half the households have no phone and the roads flood out will outrank a bigger village that's easy to reach. That's by design, not an accident of the math. **Privacy** — Village-level data (population breakdowns, shelter numbers) never leaves the local environment. The only thing that goes to a third party is the alert text itself, sent through Twilio/SendGrid to actually deliver it. **What happens when something breaks** — If a sensor fails or the script errors out, it gets logged, and the officer isn't left in the dark. The last-run JSON records exactly what happened, every time. **No black box** — Every alert spells out, in plain language, what's happening, why this village made the list, and what to actually do about it. --- ## 🛠️ If something's not working
ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'twilio' ```bash python floodready_setup.py install ```
SMS isn't arriving - Double-check `TWILIO_SID` and `TWILIO_TOKEN` — no stray spaces - On a trial account you can only text *verified* numbers — verify the recipient at `twilio.com/console/phone-numbers/verified` - Make sure `TWILIO_FROM_SMS` is your real Twilio number, country code included
Email isn't arriving - Check spam first, always - `EMAIL_FROM` needs to be verified in SendGrid under Sender Authentication - `SENDGRID_KEY` should start with `SG.` — if it doesn't, just regenerate it
WhatsApp isn't working - You need to join the Twilio sandbox first — text `join ` to `+14155238886` from the recipient's phone - `TWILIO_FROM_WA` needs the `whatsapp:` prefix, e.g. `whatsapp:+14155238886`
python not recognized on Windows ```bash py floodready_setup.py install # or python3 floodready_setup.py install ```
CSVs not found during refresh - Keep all 3 CSVs in the same folder as `floodready_setup.py` - Worst case, `run` still works off the embedded data by default
